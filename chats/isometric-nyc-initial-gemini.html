isometric nyc - gemini kickoff

I’m working on an AI art project and want to build out a design doc and explore my options. The plan is to make a giant scale isometric pixel art map of New York City (starting with Manhattan) in the style of SimCity 2000 - my plan is to go “block by block” using the google maps api, getting tiled satellite map images and converting specific sections to isometric images/tiles using nano banana image generation model - I was also considering augmenting this satellite data with street view and/or nyc open maps data (eg building footprints and data) - please help me elaborate on this idea and think through all of the challenges that I’ll need to deal with

Gemini

This is a massive, ambitious project that sits right at the intersection of geospatial engineering and generative art. The "SimCity 2000" aesthetic (2:1 isometric projection) combined with real-world NYC data is a compelling visual goal.
However, simply feeding satellite tiles into an image generator (like the "Nano Banana" model you mentioned, or a Stable Diffusion fine-tune) will likely result in a "flat" texture rather than a 3D cityscape.
Here is a breakdown of the challenges and a proposed architecture for your Design Doc to make this work.

1. The Core Challenge: Perspective Mismatch

This is your biggest hurdle.
  • Satellite Data (Nadir): Standard Google Maps tiles are top-down (nadir). They have no height information visible. If you pixelate this, it looks like a flat rug map, not a city with verticality.
  • Satellite Data (45°/Bird's Eye): Google has angled imagery, but it is in perspective, meaning parallel lines converge.
  • SimCity 2000 (Isometric): This uses orthographic projection where parallel lines never converge.
  • The Conflict: If you feed a perspective satellite photo into an AI, the AI will try to "fix" the perspective or produce warped buildings.
The Solution: The "Whitebox" Pipeline Do not rely solely on satellite imagery for the structure. You must generate a "depth map" or "guide image" using vector data first.

2. Proposed Technical Pipeline (The Design Doc)

Step 1: Data Ingestion (The Skeleton)

Instead of starting with pixels, start with NYC Open Data (PLUTO dataset) and OpenStreetMap (OSM).
  • Data: Fetch building footprints + height or num_floors attributes.
  • Process:
  1. Select a chunk of Manhattan (Lat/Long bounds).
  2. Using a library like Mapbox GL or Three.js, programmatically render these footprints as simple white blocks on a black background.
  3. Crucial Step: Set the camera to Orthographic (SimCity angle) to render this "whitebox" city.
  4. Generate two images per block:
  • Image A: The RGB satellite texture (projected onto the blocks).
  • Image B: A Depth Map (Grayscale, where closer = lighter, further = darker) or Edge Map (Canny).

Step 2: The AI Transformation (The Skin)

This is where your "Nano Banana" or similar image-gen model comes in. You cannot use simple text-to-image. You need ControlNet (if using Stable Diffusion architecture) or an Image-to-Image adapter.
  • Input: The "Whitebox" isometric render (Edge/Depth map).
  • Prompt: "Isometric pixel art, SimCity 2000 style, 16-bit, dithered shadows."
  • Mechanism: The ControlNet forces the AI to respect the building geometry derived from the NYC Open Data, while the prompt applies the "SimCity" texture and palette.

Step 3: The Street Level Augmentation

Satellite maps often obscure the street level with shadows or perspective lean.
  • Google Street View: This is likely too high-resolution and the wrong angle (ground level) to be useful for a roof-down isometric view.
  • Better Option: Use the NYC Open Data "Roadbed" shapefiles to draw the streets explicitly in your "Whitebox" render. Let the AI texture them as asphalt/concrete, rather than trying to extract them from blurry satellite photos.

3. Specific Challenges to Solve

A. The "Seams" Problem

If you generate the city in 512x512 pixel chunks, the buildings at the edge of Tile A will not line up with Tile B.
  • Solution: You must use Outpainting or Overlapping Tiles. Process a 768x768 area but only keep the center 512x512. This gives the AI context for what is "just off-screen" to ensure continuity.

B. Google Maps API vs. Cost

  • Google Maps Static API: Expensive at the scale of NYC.
  • Google Earth Studio: technically has an export feature, but licensing is restrictive for art projects.
  • Alternative: Use Mapbox Satellite or USGS High Res Orthoimagery (free/cheaper). Since you are heavily restyling it into pixel art, you don't need the proprietary clarity of Google's data.

C. The "Escher" Effect

SimCity 2000 uses a strict 2:1 pixel ratio (2 pixels over, 1 pixel up). AI models trained on general pixel art often mess this up, creating "wobbly" lines that don't look like rigid code.
  • Mitigation: You may need a post-processing script that snaps non-vertical lines to the nearest 2:1 slope, or use a pixel-art specific model checkpoint (like PixelArt diffusion or Retro Diffusion).

4. Design Doc Outline for You

Project Name: Iso-Manhattan Objective: Procedural generation of a SimCity-style map of NYC using Real World Data.
Phase 1: The Geometry Engine
  • Input: NYC PLUTO Data / OSM.
  • Output: Orthographic whitebox renders (Geometry guides).
  • Tools: Python, Mapbox/Three.js, GeoPandas.
Phase 2: The Neural Renderer
  • Input: Geometry guides + Style LoRA.
  • Processing: Stable Diffusion + ControlNet (Canny/Depth) + Pixel Art VAE.
  • Output: Raw Isometric Tiles.
Phase 3: The Assembler
  • Logic: Coordinate system transformation (Lat/Long -> Isometric Grid).
  • Stitching: Merging tiles and handling building overlap (tall buildings in the "south" of the map obscuring things "north").
